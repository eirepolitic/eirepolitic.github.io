# Revenue Investigation — Stage 1 Broad Idea Pool

## Purpose

This is Stage 1 of the October 2026 revenue-investigation workstream. The objective is to generate a large, diverse pool of online-business models that could plausibly be low-touch, low-capital, and automation-heavy before applying viability scoring.

This document is intentionally broad. It is **not** a claim that the listed ideas have validated demand or attractive economics.

## Scope and status

- Stage: `1 — broad idea generation`
- Current state: `working hypothesis / candidate pool`
- Completed: `2026-10-16`
- Next gate: owner approval before Stage 2 viability scoring
- Source plan: [`october-2026-project-work-plan.md`](/projects/notes/october-2026-project-work-plan/)

The repository currently has no dedicated `_docs/research` collection. Existing research material is stored under `_docs/notes` with the `research` tag, so this note follows that existing convention rather than creating a new site section during the revenue investigation.

## Stage 1 evidence and constraints

### Facts verified from current platform documentation

These are enabling/constraint facts only; they do not validate demand for any specific idea.

- **Low-cost direct digital selling is available.** Gumroad currently has no monthly charge and charges transaction fees; it acts as merchant of record for taxes. Stripe Payment Links can also accept one-time or recurring payments without requiring a custom checkout application. Sources: [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing), [Stripe Payment Links](https://stripe.com/en-ca/payments/payment-links).
- **Template marketplaces support direct monetization.** Notion Marketplace supports paid templates, with Notion acting as merchant of record for marketplace payments and charging an 8% + $0.40 transaction fee at the time of verification. Source: [Notion — Selling templates on Marketplace](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace).
- **Automation/web-data products have an existing marketplace channel.** Apify allows paid Actors and documents pay-per-event monetization for automation, AI-agent, and web-scraping projects. Sources: [Apify — Monetize Actors](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize), [Apify — standardizing Actor pricing](https://blog.apify.com/standardizing-actor-pricing/).
- **Newsletter monetization infrastructure exists.** beehiiv's Ad Network is available on paid plans and handles advertiser sourcing, creatives, reporting, and payments for participating publishers. Source: [beehiiv Ad Network FAQ](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/17507491038231).
- **AI-generated marketplace content is platform-constrained.** Etsy allows seller-prompted AI creations if disclosed, but does not allow AI prompt bundles under its current Creativity Standards. Source: [Etsy Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity).
- **Mass-generated commodity content is a search risk.** Google states that large-scale generative-AI pages that add little value can violate its scaled-content-abuse policy and recommends valuable, non-commodity, people-first content for generative search. Sources: [Google — generative AI content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), [Google — generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
- **Affiliate/recommendation models carry disclosure obligations.** The FTC states that material connections such as affiliate commissions should be clearly and conspicuously disclosed. Source: [FTC — Endorsements, Influencers, and Reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews).

### Assumptions for Stage 1

- The target owner prefers low ongoing interaction and does not want inventory-heavy, staff-heavy, or high-capital models.
- AI/automation may reduce operating burden but does not remove requirements for quality control, customer support, tax, privacy, IP, platform-policy, or legal compliance.
- Ideas that depend on proprietary or restricted data must be evaluated for licensing and terms-of-service constraints before implementation.
- No revenue, demand, conversion-rate, traffic, or cost assumptions below should be treated as validated until Stage 2/3.

## Duplicates and near-duplicates consolidated

The following concepts were deliberately consolidated instead of generating many cosmetic variants:

- **Templates:** spreadsheet templates, Notion templates, forms, SOPs and checklists remain separate only where the product format changes the buyer/distribution channel; dozens of industry variants were not listed.
- **Monitoring:** price monitoring, website-change monitoring, regulatory monitoring and restock alerts share infrastructure, but remain distinct because payer, value and data/legal constraints differ.
- **Directories:** vendor, resource, event, job and API directories were kept as distinct business mechanisms; geography/industry variants were not multiplied.
- **Lead generation:** local service leads, tenders, grants and B2B trigger events were separated by acquisition/data mechanism rather than by every possible vertical.
- **Content businesses:** newsletter, database/wiki, calculators, comparisons and data-visualization publications were separated; generic "AI blog" variants were intentionally excluded.
- **Automated research:** research briefs, data feeds and monitoring alerts were separated by deliverable; many possible subject-matter niches were not duplicated.
- **Micro-SaaS:** the pool lists different workflow primitives (deadlines, monitoring, quoting, intake, reporting) rather than many industry-specific clones.
- **Marketplace automation:** Apify Actors, API wrappers, workflow packs and browser extensions are separate distribution/product forms, not multiple copies of the same scraper idea.

These are **not yet shortlisted winners**. They are the candidates that currently deserve comparative scoring because they combine a clear payer, meaningful automation potential, low initial capital requirements, and an MVP that can usually be tested without building a large organization.

### Fast-test candidates worth keeping in the Stage 2 comparison

Three additional ideas merit Stage 2 scoring primarily because they can be tested unusually cheaply and quickly, even if their upside may be lower:

- **A1 — Operational spreadsheet/model packs**: very small MVP; direct sales can validate a niche before software development.
- **A2 — Notion/knowledge-work systems**: existing marketplace distribution and merchant-of-record infrastructure reduce launch friction.
- **B2 — Data-backed market brief**: can validate willingness to pay for a dataset/insight before building an API or recurring platform.

## Ideas intentionally not promoted yet

No idea is formally rejected in Stage 1, but several families should enter Stage 2 with caution rather than enthusiasm:

- generic AI-written SEO sites, because Google explicitly emphasizes non-commodity value and warns about scaled low-value AI content;
- broad affiliate sites, because they depend heavily on search/platform distribution and carry disclosure obligations;
- generic print-on-demand/AI-art catalogs, because differentiation, platform policy and IP review are material constraints;
- broad consumer apps, because customer acquisition can dominate otherwise-low infrastructure cost;
- two-sided marketplaces, because they can require substantially more human acquisition effort than their software footprint suggests.

## Stage 2 proposed method

If approved, Stage 2 should score the 15 recommended/fast-test candidates against the agreed criteria:

- startup cost;
- time to first revenue;
- ongoing human effort;
- automation potential;
- legal/platform risk; and
- expected upside.

The scoring pass should use explicit evidence where available, mark estimates separately from facts, and eliminate weak candidates before any deep investigation.
