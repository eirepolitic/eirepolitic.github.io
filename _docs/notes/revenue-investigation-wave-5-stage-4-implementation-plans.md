# Wave 5 — Stage 4 Implementation Plans

## Guiding principle

The business should sell through **high-intent discovery → free sample/preview → self-serve checkout → automated delivery → optional repeat purchase**. No routine cold email or lead chasing. Discovery is the central unproven dependency; publishing a page or marketplace listing alone does not produce traffic.

Prices are hypotheses in CAD; revenue arithmetic is gross, before payment fees, hosting, taxes, support and refunds. No Stage 5 work is authorized.

## W5-1 RFP checklist generator (rank 1)

**Customer:** occasional Canadian public-tender bidders. **Problem:** long solicitation documents hide mandatory forms, submission conditions and deadlines. **Product:** upload PDF/DOCX; download source-referenced Excel matrix with requirement, source page, category, due date, status and uncertainty flags. **Free alternative:** general-purpose AI and manual spreadsheet. **Why pay:** repeatable structured output and visible citations, not guaranteed compliance.

**Pricing test:** CA$29 standard, CA$59 large; free example from public RFP. At CA$29, 35 orders/month ≈ CA$1,015 gross. **Discovery:** useful public example pages for specific tender categories, high-intent search queries, optional listing in appropriate document-tool directories. Avoid mass-generated thin SEO pages. **Acquisition funnel:** search visitor → real sample preview → upload/size estimate → secure checkout → extraction → user download → deletion schedule → optional follow-up for repeat buyers. **Automation:** file validation, extraction, page mapping, workbook generation, payment, receipt, delivery, deletion, error alerts. **Human QA gate:** test 10 varied public RFPs, audit all mandatory items/deadlines, fail closed on low-confidence sections; never promise 100% capture. **Budget:** CA$0–$150 prelaunch excluding time; ongoing per-document AI/hosting/payment costs. **Owner burden target:** <30 min/week after stable launch, but higher during accuracy QA. **Go:** 3 paid inbound purchases or 5 genuine checkout-intent signals after demonstrable targeted traffic; zero critical misses in controlled QA before selling. **Kill:** critical omissions, no discoverability, or review cost greater than margin. **Risks:** confidentiality, liability, search competition.

## W5-2 Canadian importer lookup (rank 2)

**Customer:** exporters and trade researchers searching for Canadian importers by product category. **Product:** dated, source-linked category report, clean CSV/XLSX, filters and transparent annual-data caveats. **Why pay over free official database:** time savings, useful grouping and usable downloads. **Pricing test:** CA$49/category or CA$99 bundle; at CA$49, 21 orders/month ≈ CA$1,029 gross. **Discovery:** substantive category-specific pages with useful free counts, methodology and sample rows; search targeting exact importer categories; optional free limited preview. **Funnel:** category search → free preview → pay → instant report download → optional new annual edition notification (opt-in). **Automation:** ingest public data subject to permitted reuse, normalize names/categories, generate previews, checkout, delivery and update alerts. **Validation gate:** verify official data licensing/reuse and current coverage, build a *manual mock preview* for 10 categories, test whether at least two strangers buy without direct solicitation; do not fabricate purchase results. **Budget:** CA$0–$100 plus payment/hosting. **Owner burden:** <30 min/week after annual update pipeline stabilizes. **Go:** 2 real inbound purchases and evidence of non-branded relevant traffic. **Kill:** no willingness to pay versus free source, weak category demand, restrictive reuse conditions or stale/unhelpful results. **Risks:** free competition, freshness, search-indexing dependence.

## W5-3 Sponsor report generator (rank 3)

**Customer:** independent newsletter publishers running repeat paid sponsorships. **Product:** self-serve form for sponsor name, issue URL, publisher-supplied opens/clicks, screenshot and logo → branded PDF/share link. Never invent metrics. **Why pay over free Canva/screenshots:** time saved and consistent repeat reporting. **Pricing test:** CA$19/report or CA$39/month for 5 reports; 53 one-off orders/month ≈ CA$1,007 gross, or 26 subscribers at CA$39 ≈ CA$1,014 gross. **Discovery:** SEO around newsletter sponsorship reports, a genuinely useful free example/template, creator-tool directories, non-spam participation in publisher communities. **Funnel:** sample → free watermarked preview → pay → final export → opt-in monthly plan. **Automation:** form, template rendering, checkout, delivery, saved brand kit, receipts, usage limits. **Validation gate:** 5 publishers voluntarily create a second report and 3 pay; track self-serve discovery sources. **Budget:** CA$0–$75 plus payment fees and PDF hosting. **Owner burden:** <20 min/week if inputs are structured. **Go:** 3 paid repeat users with evidence of inbound discovery. **Kill:** publishers prefer free exports/screenshots, reports needed too infrequently or customer acquisition cost exceeds margin. **Risks:** low willingness to pay and platform-native sponsor reporting.

## Cross-cutting inbound acquisition and monitoring pipeline

1. **Demand verification (prebuild):** collect relevant search queries and competing result pages; inspect app-store categories/directories and free alternatives. Record observed evidence, not assumed traffic.
2. **One honest landing page:** clear example output, price hypothesis, FAQ and visible limitations; only after development is authorized. A waitlist may gauge interest but is not a paid order.
3. **Self-serve preview:** sample or watermarked output, without forcing a sales call or email collection.
4. **Checkout and delivery:** automated payment, access control, receipts, output and refund/error handling.
5. **Inbound attribution:** track page visits, sample views, checkout starts, paid conversions and repeat purchases without intrusive tracking.
6. **Support triage:** automatic FAQ and error routing; owner sees only payment issues, critical accuracy concerns and meaningful requests.
7. **Decision dashboard:** visitors → previews → checkout starts → paid purchases → repeat use; measure gross margin and owner minutes per order.

**Legal/privacy:** transparent processing and retention, source attribution, payment/tax obligations, secure handling of customer files, opt-in follow-up and no unsolicited bulk outreach. For RFP documents, do not retain confidential content longer than needed or use it for training without authorization.

## Combined recommendation

The highest-value lesson from Wave 5 is **not** that an app marketplace is a free customer-acquisition engine. It is that a narrow, useful self-serve result can be sold directly to someone already searching for that result. RFP matrices, importer reports and sponsor proof reports have clearer paid outputs than a $9/month micro-app requiring more than 100 active customers.

No development, deployment, payment setup, marketing campaign or customer outreach has been performed. The next gate is user authorization to begin Stage 5 on a selected idea; further research is also an option.
